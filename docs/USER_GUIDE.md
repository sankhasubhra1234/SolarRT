# SolarRT user guide

SolarRT shows where the aurora (northern and southern lights) is likely right now, how active Earth's
magnetic field is, and what that means where you are. It works the same on the website, the Windows app and
the Android app.

## First start

SolarRT asks how to find you:
- **📍 Use my current location**: your device's location (most accurate). Allow it when asked.
- **Use approximate location**: estimated from your internet connection, accurate to the nearest town.
- **🔍 Search for a place**: type a town or place name.

Your choice is remembered on your device. Change it any time with **◎ Locate me**, the search box, or
**📌 Pick on map**, which lets you click one spot. Use **↺ back to my location** to return.

## The screen

| Part | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Map colours** | Chance of aurora overhead: green (low) → yellow → orange → red (very likely). Updated about every 5 min |
| **Dark shading** | The night side of Earth. You can only see aurora in the dark |
| **Your location card** | Chance overhead, whether it may be visible on your horizon (≤ 800 km away), sky darkness, weather, local time |
| **Weather strip** | Cloud cover for the next 24 h, with night hours marked; plus the next dark & clear hour |
| **Kp index** | Global geomagnetic activity now, the last 3 h, the 24 h maximum, the forecast peak, and a 3-day chart (G1+ = storm) |
| **Regional aurora activity** | Best viewing regions ranked by current chance. Tap one to zoom there |
| **Clock (top bar)** | Time at your location. Tap to switch every time shown to UTC |

## Map buttons

- **🛰 ISS**: the International Space Station live, with its track (one orbit) and the area that can see it.
  Use *Follow on map* to keep it centred.
- **☁ Clouds**: worldwide satellite cloud image, updated every 3 hours.
- **Layers** (top right): turn the aurora forecast and night shading on or off.

## Aurora alerts

1. Switch on **🔔 Aurora alerts**.
2. Choose what triggers an alert: the chance at your location reaches X %, *or* aurora on your horizon,
   *or* Kp reaches a level.
3. Choose the sky condition: **any**, **only when dark**, or **only when dark and clear** (set the maximum
   cloud cover and, optionally, at least 10 km visibility).
4. Press **Send test alert** to check notifications work.

You get at most one alert every 3 hours, unless activity gets stronger. **Alerts are checked while
SolarRT is open.** Phones pause apps in the background to save battery, so keep SolarRT on screen, or open it
regularly, on an aurora night. Background alerts are on the roadmap.

## Tips for seeing the aurora

- Get away from city lights and look toward the pole: north in the Northern Hemisphere, south in the
  Southern.
- Best hours are usually 21:00–03:00 local time. Let your eyes adjust for 15–20 minutes.
- A phone camera on night mode often shows colour before your eyes do.

## Theme and help

- **◐ Auto / ☀ Light / ☾ Dark** (top bar) changes the colour theme.
- **FAQ** is at the bottom of the side panel, and also linked in the bottom bar.
- **About** explains the science and the data sources.
- **💬 Feedback**: pick a face, add a comment if you like, and send. Thank you!

## Getting the apps

- **Windows:** run `SolarRT-Setup-2.0.0.exe`. No admin rights are needed.
- **Android:** install the SolarRT APK (or get it from Google Play once it's published).
- **iPhone:** open the website in Safari → Share → **Add to Home Screen**.
