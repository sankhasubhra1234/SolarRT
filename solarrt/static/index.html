      ❓ Frequently asked questions

      What does the coloured map show?

      NOAA's OVATION aurora forecast: the chance of seeing an aurora directly overhead at each place, about 30–90 minutes ahead. Green is a low chance, then yellow and orange; red means very likely. It updates about every 5 minutes.

      What is the Kp index, and what Kp do I need?

      Kp (0–9) measures how disturbed Earth's magnetic field is. Higher Kp pushes the aurora further from the poles. Very roughly, by magnetic latitude:

      Kp1–23–45 (G1)6 (G2)7 (G3)8–9
      Reaches about66°+62°56°54°50°45° and below

      Magnetic latitude differs from geographic latitude (Europe sees aurora at lower latitudes than North America at the same Kp). The map and "chance here" already take this into account.

      The map shows aurora, so why does it say 0% for me?

      "Chance here" is for the sky straight above you. An aurora further north (or south) can still be seen low on the horizon from up to ~800 km away, which SolarRT reports separately. You also need darkness: in daylight or twilight you won't see it even if it's there.

      When and how should I look?

      Go somewhere dark, away from city lights, and look toward the pole (north in the Northern Hemisphere, south in the Southern). The best time is usually 21:00–03:00 local time. Give your eyes 15–20 minutes to adjust. Phone cameras often show colour your eyes can't see yet.

      How accurate is the forecast?

      It's a model driven by live solar-wind measurements, so it's good for the next hour but can change quickly. The 3-day Kp forecast is less certain. Treat it as a guide and check again before heading out.

      How do aurora alerts work? Do they work when the app is closed?

      Choose what should trigger an alert (aurora chance here, aurora on your horizon, or a Kp level) and whether it should wait for a dark, or dark and clear, sky. Alerts are checked while SolarRT is open. Phones pause apps in the background to save battery, and a closed app can't check at all, so keep SolarRT open on an aurora night.

      What do the clouds and weather show?

      The ☁ Clouds layer is a worldwide infrared satellite image from EUMETSAT, updated every 3 hours. Bright areas are cold, high cloud. The weather in your location card (cloud cover, visibility, the next dark and clear hour) is an hourly forecast from Open-Meteo.

      What are the ISS line and ring?

      The 🛰 ISS button shows the International Space Station live. The solid line is where it has been, the dashed line is where it's going (one full orbit), and the ring is the area from which it's above the horizon. To see it with your own eyes, the ISS needs to be in sunlight (☀ Sunlit) while your sky is dark, usually shortly after sunset or before sunrise.

      Why is my location wrong?

      "Approximate location" is estimated from your internet connection and can be off by tens of kilometres. Press "◎ Locate me" to use your device's location, search for your town, or use "📌 Pick on map".

      Which time zone are the times in?

      By default, the local time of the place you've selected. Click the clock at the top to switch to UTC, the standard for space-weather data. The Kp chart always uses UTC, because Kp is measured in fixed 3-hour UTC slots.

      What data does SolarRT collect?

      Your chosen location stays on your device. To fetch the forecast for it, the app sends the coordinates to the SolarRT server, which passes them to the weather and place-search services without storing them. Feedback you choose to send (rating, comment and optional contact) is stored so it can be read. There are no ads, trackers or accounts. See the privacy policy for details.

      Where does the data come from? Is it free?

      NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (aurora forecast and Kp), Open-Meteo (weather), EUMETSAT (clouds), CelesTrak (ISS orbit), OpenStreetMap (place names) and Esri (map). SolarRT is free to use.

      It says "unavailable" or shows old data. What's wrong?

      Usually one of the data services is briefly down. SolarRT keeps showing the last good data and refreshes automatically. Check your internet connection if nothing loads at all.

      Can I get SolarRT on my phone or computer?

      Yes. There's an Android app and a Windows desktop app with the same features. On iPhone, use the website in Safari and choose Share → Add to Home Screen.

      Welcome to SolarRT

      To show your chances of seeing the aurora, the local sky conditions and alerts, SolarRT needs to know where you are.

      Your location stays in this browser or app on your device. You can change it any time with "Locate me", the search box or "Pick on map".

      About SolarRT

      SolarRT shows where the aurora is likely right now, how disturbed Earth's magnetic field is (Kp index), and what that means for where you are.

      Regional aurora map

      The coloured overlay is NOAA's OVATION Prime model: the probability of seeing an aurora overhead, on a 1°×1° grid, for about 30–90 minutes ahead. It refreshes roughly every 5 minutes.

      Kp index & storm levels

      Kp (0–9) measures global geomagnetic disturbance every 3 hours. Kp 5 and above is a geomagnetic storm on NOAA's scale: G1 Minor (5), G2 Moderate (6), G3 Strong (7), G4 Severe (8), G5 Extreme (9). The higher the Kp, the further from the poles the aurora reaches.

      Your chances

      "Chance here" is the OVATION probability at your location. A bright aurora can also be seen low on the horizon from several hundred kilometres away, so the app also checks within 800 km. You also need a dark, clear sky — the app tells you when the Sun is too high.

      ISS tracker

      Press the 🛰 ISS button on the map to follow the International Space Station live. Its position is calculated on the server from its latest published orbit (from CelesTrak) using the SGP4 model, to within about a kilometre.

      Clouds & weather

      The ☁ Clouds map layer is EUMETSAT's worldwide infrared satellite composite (updated every 3 hours); bright = cold, high cloud. The weather for your location (cloud cover, visibility, the next dark and clear hour) comes from Open-Meteo.

      Aurora alerts

      Alerts are checked while SolarRT is open. Choose what triggers them (aurora chance here, aurora on your horizon, or a Kp level) and whether they should wait for a dark sky, a dark and clear sky, or ignore the weather. You get at most one alert per three hours unless activity gets stronger.

      Your location & privacy

      On first visit SolarRT asks how to find you: your device's current location (with your permission), an approximate location estimated from your IP address (via geojs.io), or a place you search for. Your chosen location is stored only in this browser or app.

      Local time

      The clock in the top bar shows the time at your selected location, in that place's own time zone. Click it to switch every time in the app between local time and UTC, the standard for space-weather data. The Kp chart always uses UTC, because Kp is measured in fixed 3-hour UTC slots.
